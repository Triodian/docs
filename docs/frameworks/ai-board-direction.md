This framework helps a board move from asking "what artificial intelligence (AI) do we use?" to a resolved position on AI. The position covers:

- where the board wants AI used
- what it will not accept
- which decisions must come back to it
- what information may go into which AI tools
- what harm it will not tolerate
- what it expects management to report
- who is accountable.

The board works through 10 steps, B0 to B9. Its board adviser and its general counsel (GC) run the sessions. Triodian provides the structure and records the positions. The framework runs in one of 2 modes, chosen at set-up.

| Item | Position |
| --- | --- |
| Question answered | Where does the board want AI used, what will it not accept, and what must it see and decide itself? |
| Who decides | The board. The positions are the board's, proposed by the board and management |
| Who runs it | The board adviser runs the sessions. The GC, in-house or fractional, acts as counsel. Triodian supplies the steps and scales and records positions |
| Method | 10 steps, B0 to B9, worked through in one workshop and resolved at the next board meeting |
| Modes | **Standalone**, for organisations outside prudential regulation: the steps produce the whole appetite. **Connected**, for entities regulated by the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA), the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) or the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS): the steps produce board directions the AI Risk Appetite engagement must stay within |
| Duration | Standalone: 2 to 4 weeks, one workshop of about 3 hours. Connected: a 2-hour directions workshop before the AI Risk Appetite sessions and an approval session after them |
| Inputs | Existing risk appetite or risk register, delegations of authority, privacy policy, any AI policy, the AI tools in use, an Atlas matter brief and, if connected, the AI Use Assessment register summary |
| Outputs | Standalone: AI risk appetite statement, acceptable-use policy outline, board minute, 90-day action list, automated-decision worklist, confidentiality position. Connected: board directions paper, then the board view |

**Standing rule.** The appetite is the board's. What the organisation is obliged to have, whether a stated position meets that obligation, and what directors' duties require are questions for counsel. Triodian and the board adviser supply structure and record positions, and give no view on what the appetite should be.

**Second standing rule.** The framework records facts about AI use and the board's positions. It holds no client data, no member, customer or client personal information and no confidential documents. It records the names of the people taking part, as the privacy notice describes.

## 2 modes

The mode is chosen at set-up, based on whether the organisation is regulated. It cannot be changed after B1 is recorded. If an organisation becomes regulated, it starts a connected engagement and uses its standalone statement as a starting point.

|  | Standalone | Connected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| For | Private companies, not-for-profits, listed companies outside prudential regulation | APRA-regulated entities, ASIC licensees, MAS-regulated financial institutions |
| What the board sets | The whole appetite, in plain words | Directions: posture, red lines, reserved decisions, confidentiality, harm, reporting, accountability |
| Detailed positions | None; the statement is the appetite | Set in the AI Risk Appetite engagement, decisions 1 to 8, and checked against the directions (check K11) |
| Evidence of AI in use | A management inventory of 10 to 20 lines (B1) | The AI Use Assessment register summary |
| Scales | Plain-language versions | The authority tiers A1 to A6 and consequence levels C1 to C5 |
| Ends with | A board resolution and statement | Board directions now; approval of the AI risk appetite statement after the AI Risk Appetite engagement |

### What connected mode passes to AI Risk Appetite

Each step from B2 to B8 produces one direction. AI Risk Appetite reads each direction on the decision it affects; a position that goes past a direction fails check K11 and cannot form part of the working appetite until a reason is recorded and the board revisits the direction.

| Direction | From step | Read on decision | How K11 tests it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BD1 Posture by area | B2 | 1 authority | A ceiling in an area marked Avoid above A1, or in an area marked Cautious above A2 |
| BD2 Red lines | B3 | 1 authority; 6 location | A ceiling, condition or location marking that would permit a red-lined use |
| BD3 Reserved decisions and the fast path | B4 | 1 authority; 4 shortfall tolerance | A ceiling at or above a reserved level with no route back to the board; an exception route that bypasses the board |
| BD4 Confidential information | B5 | Read by AI Use Assessment as context | Recorded on each register entry that reaches the class |
| BD5 Harm not tolerated | B6 | 2 consequence thresholds | A threshold looser than the harm the board said it will not tolerate |
| BD6 Reporting expected | B7 | 7 triggers; the measures | A trigger or measure the board asked for that is missing or slower than asked |
| BD7 Accountability | B8 | Owners of every position | A position owned by someone other than the executive the board named |

### What standalone mode passes to Confidentiality Exposure Assessment

B5 records, for each class of confidential information and each kind of AI tool, whether use is permitted, permitted with conditions, or not permitted. A later matter assessment imports the position and shows a suggested required rung on each channel; counsel confirms or replaces it, and only counsel sets that field.

| Board's position | Own estate (B1) | Adviser's estate (B2) | Personal device (B3) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Not permitted | N5 containment | N2 attestation | N1 direction |
| Permitted with conditions | N4 record | N2 attestation | N1 direction |
| Permitted | N1 direction | N1 direction | N1 direction |

## Roles

| Party | Role | Where it stops |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board | Decides every position; resolves the statement or the directions | |
| Chair | Chairs the workshop; alone records the resolution at B9 | |
| Directors | Propose and test positions | |
| CEO, or the accountable executive | Supplies the inventory at B1; proposes positions; owns the 90-day action list | |
| Technology owner (usually the CIO) and AI governance team | Supply and maintain the inventory, what existing policies and thresholds say, and draft measures; the technology owner confirms each line and draft measure | Propose or record no board position |
| CRO (connected only) | Brings the risk function's view; owns the AI Risk Appetite engagement that follows | |
| General counsel | Acts as counsel; alone answers the legal questions logged; advises on what the board must set and what the outputs may say; drafts the acceptable-use policy from the outline | Proposes no position |
| Board adviser | Runs the sessions: agenda, pre-read, workshop, minute | Proposes no position; gives no legal advice |
| Triodian | Supplies the steps, scales and position file; records; runs the checks | States no view on what the appetite should be or whether it meets any obligation |
| Area owners | Each confirms the positions for their area | |

Where the GC also acts as counsel, the second agreement at B0 comes from a named executive or director, and every output records that the GC held both roles.

**Rules of engagement.**

- Positions come from the board and management. In B2 to B8, only a director, the chief executive officer (CEO) or, in connected mode, the chief risk officer (CRO) can propose a position.
- Legal questions are written down as numbered questions to counsel. They are not decided in the workshop.
- Counsel decides the privilege position. The workshop record and drafts are handled through counsel on that basis. The privilege marking appears on the first line of every output.
- Every position is recorded once, with who proposed it, any dissent and any condition.

## The 10 steps

| # | Step | Question to the board | Answered as | Connected: direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B0 | Set-up, briefing and pre-read | What applies to us, and what do directors need to know first? | Scope, mode, privilege position, matter brief; the director briefing and check questions | |
| B1 | Where AI is used | What AI do we use now, directly and through providers? | An inventory of 10 to 20 lines, each with authority level and the automated-decision flag | Register summary |
| B2 | Goals and posture | What should AI governance achieve, and how cautious are we in each area? | Goals, each with an owner and a measure; a posture, Avoid to Pursue, per area | BD1 |
| B3 | Red lines | Which uses of AI do we not accept at all? | A list of uses, each testable against the inventory | BD2 |
| B4 | Reserved to the board | Which AI decisions must come back to us, and which may management approve quickly? | A list of events, each with who brings it and when; the fast path (BC2) | BD3 |
| B5 | Confidential information | What information may go into which AI tools? | Permitted, with conditions, or not permitted, for each class and tool kind | BD4 |
| B6 | Harm we will not tolerate | What failure would be unacceptable to customers, staff or the organisation? | A statement and threshold per kind of harm | BD5 |
| B7 | What we expect to hear | What should management report to us about AI, and how fast? | 3 to 5 measures, and the events reported between meetings | BD6 |
| B8 | Who is accountable | Which committee, director and executive own AI? | A named owner for the whole and for each area | BD7 |
| B9 | Resolve and review | Do we resolve this, and when do we look again? | A resolution, a review interval and change events | Approval session |

In standalone mode B0 to B8 are taken in one workshop and B9 at the next board meeting. In connected mode B0 to B8 are taken before the AI Risk Appetite sessions and B9 follows them.

### Scales

**Posture (B2)**

| Code | Posture | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | Avoid | We do not want AI used in this area beyond what assists a person, for now |
| S2 | Cautious | We accept AI that assists or recommends; a person decides |
| S3 | Open | We accept AI that decides within stated limits, where we can see what it did |
| S4 | Pursue | We want AI used here and accept that it may act with less oversight, within our red lines and reserved decisions |

**Authority (B1, B4)**: the plain names of the AI Use Assessment tiers. A1 assists, A2 recommends, A3 decides, A4 moves money, A5 acts unattended, A6 changes systems.

**Kinds of harm (B6)**: H1 customers or members; H2 staff and applicants; H3 the organisation's money; H4 operations; H5 legal and regulatory standing; H6 reputation and trust. In connected mode H1 and H2 map to C4, H3 to C3, H4 to C5, H5 to C2; H6 drives triggers only.

**Confidential information classes (B5)**: G1 public; G2 internal; G3 commercial in confidence; G4 personal information; G5 restricted (health information, legal advice, export-controlled or security-classified material, trade secrets); G6 board and transaction. Descriptive only; counsel determines their legal character. **Tool kinds (B5)** are the Confidentiality Exposure Assessment's channel kinds K1 to K7.

### What each step records

- **B0.** Organisation, entity type and jurisdiction; mode with the reason; areas of the business; obligations in scope imported from the matter brief by tier, with no statement of what applies; privilege marking as counsel instructs; agreement to scope by the GC and the chair, or a named second approver; board dates. The director briefing is built from the matter brief and the sector pack. It covers AI in plain terms, the obligations in scope, how directors' duties apply to AI, common ways AI fails and a few questions to check understanding. The app records when each director completes it. Only that director and the facilitator can see the director's answers. Directors who have not completed it are listed at B9, and the chair decides whether to go ahead.
- **B1.** One line per AI use: use, tool and provider, area, who uses it, authority, information it sees, tool kind, whether it makes or substantially contributes to a decision about an individual using personal information (field 8), owner, source. A use nobody can describe is recorded as not known, with who will find out and by when. Every line marked yes at field 8 raises a question to counsel and goes to the automated-decision worklist.
- **B2.** Goals with owners and measures that B7 picks up; one posture per area, starting from the posture that matches the highest authority level B1 records there.
- **B3.** A list of red lines, each naming the use, the area and whether it applies to the organisation, its providers or both; each must be testable against an inventory line. A list of uses boards commonly consider is shown only if the board asks and is not a recommendation.
- **B4.** Reserved events: a new use at or above a stated authority level; a new use in an area marked Avoid; an exception to a red line; a new provider whose AI sees G5 or G6 information; a material AI incident; a change to the statement or measures. For each, before or after, and who brings it. The fast path: uses at A1 and A2 with consequence C1 or C2 approved by management within a stated number of days.
- **B5.** One marking per class and tool kind: P permitted, C permitted with conditions written beside it, N not permitted. Moving to a more sensitive class a marking may tighten but not loosen. A condition must be something management can show.
- **B6.** For each kind of harm, what the board will not tolerate and the threshold, taken from existing incident, loss and complaint thresholds unchanged where they exist. A material AI incident is an AI failure that meets or exceeds any threshold.
- **B7.** Measures reported at each meeting, and events reported between meetings. Each has a limit, an owner and a time.
  - Measures at each meeting: AI uses in the inventory, and new uses since the last report; uses that cross a red line or exceed the posture without approval; material AI incidents; known uses of information marked N; overdue actions; days from request to approval; share of AI use on approved tools.
  - Events reported between meetings: a material incident; a red line crossed; a regulator or counterparty raising AI use; a provider changing the AI it uses in an area marked Avoid or Cautious.
- **B8.** The committee that oversees AI; the director who leads, if any; the executive accountable overall; an owner per area; who may approve an exception and within what limit.
- **B9.** The resolution wording, meeting and minute reference, review interval (at least annual, with the board's risk review) and change events: a material AI incident; a new use at or above the reserved level; a change in regulatory position; new law or guidance as counsel advises; the interval passing.

## Outputs

Every output:

- is marked draft until the chair records the resolution at B9
- shows the privilege marking on its first line
- names the version it came from
- has an evidence line naming the instruments it relates to.

| # | Output | Mode | For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BO1 | AI risk appetite statement (2 pages) | Standalone | The board, to resolve |
| BO2 | Acceptable-use policy outline | Standalone | The GC, to draft the policy |
| BO3 | Board minute | Both | The company secretary |
| BO4 | 90-day action list | Both | The CEO |
| BO5 | Automated-decision worklist | Both | The GC |
| BO6 | Board directions paper | Connected | The board, then the AI Risk Appetite engagement |
| BO7 | Board view; the evidence index | Connected | The board at each meeting; the regulator response |
| BO8 | Workshop record | Both | Working paper, handled as counsel directs |
| | Engagement plan; director education record | Both | The practitioner; the board |

**Language rules for every output.**

- State what was recorded and who proposed it.
- Do not state what the law requires, whether a duty is met or what the board should decide.
- Do not name any product.
- Do not include personal information, client data or confidential content. Describe uses and information by type.
- Use "red line", "reserved" and "not tolerated" only for positions the board has recorded.

## The position file

The 10 positions BP0 to BP9 are issued as one file (`triodian.bd.v1`) with a version, status and date. The directions are issued as `triodian.bd.directions.v1`, and the confidentiality position as `triodian.bd.confidentiality.v1`. The status is draft, directions issued (connected mode) or resolved.

- Any change creates a new version. The other apps record which version they read.
- Directions are never shared while they are being edited.
- If a position is not set, the default is used, and the outputs say which positions used the default.
- The file contains no client data.

## Checks before the outputs leave draft

| # | Check | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BK1 | Complete | A position is blank and not recorded as not set with an owner and date |
| BK2 | Ordered | A B5 marking loosens moving to a more sensitive class |
| BK3 | Consistent | An area's posture accepts a level of authority a red line or reserved decision rules out there, with no reason recorded |
| BK4 | Testable | A red line or condition cannot be tested against an inventory line; red lines record what they were tested against |
| BK5 | Owned | An area, goal, measure or event has no named owner or measure |
| BK6 | Covered | An inventory line sees G3 to G6 information through a tool kind with no B5 marking for that class |
| BK7 | Referred | An inventory line marked yes at field 8 has no question to counsel |

**Reality note.** Separately from the checks, every inventory line that already crosses a red line, exceeds its area's posture or uses information marked N is reported. It is not a failure: it tells the board its positions will produce actions on the first day, so they are taken knowingly. Each goes to the 90-day action list.

## Governance

- The accountable executive owns the statement or directions.
- The board, or its committee under delegation, resolves them.
- Each measure has the owner named at B7.
- Exceptions are approved as set at B8. They have a time limit and are reported.
- Changes to the measures go to the next meeting. Changes to the statement go to the board.
- The statement is reviewed at the interval set at B9, and after any change event.

The resolved statement or directions, the minute and each version of the position file are kept together. The workshop record and the question log are working papers. Counsel decides how they are handled.

## Starting lists

**Areas of the business (standalone, where the organisation has no list):** customer service and communications; sales and marketing; people and hiring; finance and payments; operations and production; product, research and engineering; procurement and suppliers; legal, contracts and compliance; IT and security; board and executive work. Connected mode and sector packs use the pack's functions.

**Uses boards commonly consider at B3** (shown only if asked; inclusion is not a recommendation): AI making a final decision to hire, dismiss or discipline with no person reviewing; AI-written content sent to customers as if a person wrote it; G6 information in any tool outside the organisation's estate; AI holding credentials to make payments or change production systems unattended; biometric identification or monitoring of staff or customers; personal information used to train an external model; AI monitoring staff communications without notice.
