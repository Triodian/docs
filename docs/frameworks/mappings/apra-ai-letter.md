APRA's letter to industry on artificial intelligence (30 April 2026) sets "at a minimum" expectations for how APRA-regulated entities govern AI, under four headings: the board; security and resilience; governance; providers; and assurance. It names no prudential standard and has no paragraph numbers, so the suite cites it by section and bullet, B1 to A4, nineteen in all. APRA says it requires risk appetite, oversight and accountability for AI and will pursue enforcement where entities fall short.

## What it asks, by bullet

| Ref | Expectation |
| --- | --- |
| B1 | Board AI literacy sufficient to set direction and challenge, without overreliance on vendor presentations |
| B2 | An AI strategy consistent with risk appetite and tolerances, with monitoring including third-party dependencies and clearly defined triggers |
| S1 | AI reliance assessed for resilience; credible fallbacks where AI supports critical operations |
| S2 | Security controls for AI threats, including controls over agentic and autonomous workflows |
| S3 | Security testing of AI-generated code, components and libraries |
| S4 | Third-party and concentration implications of common platforms and providers |
| G1 | AI policy, standards, guidance and reporting lines |
| G2 | Ownership and accountability across the lifecycle |
| G3 | An inventory of AI tooling and use cases |
| G4 | Human involvement for high-risk decisions |
| G5 | Staff training on AI use, misuse, limits and secure practice |
| P1 | The full AI supply chain mapped, including third and fourth parties |
| P2 | Contracts giving transparency, auditability and assurance over AI services |
| P3 | Model behaviour, material changes, performance and outcomes understood across the lifecycle |
| P4 | Concentration risk, failure scenarios, and credible substitution, portability or exit |
| A1 | Recognised control frameworks, control libraries and change control |
| A2 | Integrated assurance across cyber, data, model performance, resilience, privacy and conduct |
| A3 | Second line and internal audit able to assess AI independently |
| A4 | Assessment before deployment and through the lifecycle; continuous, proportionate monitoring |

## Where the suite maps

Every output in an Australian regulated-chain engagement carries an "Evidence for" line naming its letter bullet (outside Australia the line carries ISO/IEC 42001 only), and the evidence index (output BO7 in connected AI Board Direction) gathers the artefacts from all three apps by bullet, showing "none yet" where there is nothing. The mapping file is `public/shared/evidence.js`, the same in each app.

| Bullets | Where the evidence comes from |
| --- | --- |
| B1 | The director briefing at B0 and the director education record |
| B2 | The eight decisions of AI Risk Appetite, which answer the board bullet almost line by line; triggers in the calibration set; board directions and check K11 |
| S1, S4, P4 | Critical operations in Lite scope; measure M10 (critical operations depending on one AI provider); decision 4's limit, suspend or replace position |
| G2, G3, G4 | The AI Use Assessment register (staff, provider and fourth-party AI); the B1 inventory; the authority scale A1 to A6 and the human-checkpoint control BC1 |
| P1, P2, P3 | The seven provider questions; the evidence ladder E0 to E6 (E3 incident notification, E4 audit rights, E5 continuous monitoring); findings F1 to F4 |
| A1 to A4 | The classification record, review dates and the evidence index; A1 and A3 also by the versioned content and rules |
| S2, S3, G1, G5 | Not delivered by the apps; recorded as gaps in the index for the entity's other tools and training to close |
