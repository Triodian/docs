CISA and the sector risk management agencies (FERC and NERC for the bulk electric system, TSA for pipelines and rail, EPA for water) govern AI in critical infrastructure through cybersecurity standards and directives. The pack `usCriticalInfrastructure` is built on the corporate critical infrastructure baseline; a government-run operator chooses the government baseline.


> **Status.** US support is being completed: the five sector packs are built, and the worked examples and the installer's US option follow. See [United States](/concepts/united-states).

## The instruments

| Instrument | Tier | What it reaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NERC CIP reliability standards | Binding | Access, change and supply-chain controls, which reach AI tools inside CIP environments |
| TSA security directives (pipelines, rail) | Binding | Cybersecurity implementation plans and assessment |
| CIRCIA final rule (sent for review 1 October 2026) | Anticipated | 72-hour incident and 24-hour ransom-payment reports across all 16 sectors once in force |
| CISA AI and OT security guidance; NIST AI RMF | Expected; adopted | Principles for AI in operational technology |

## Where the suite maps

| Element | App | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI tools inside regulated OT environments authorised, inventoried and monitored | AI Use Assessment; AI Board Direction | The register; B1 inventory; authority A6 and agent controls BC1 |
| CIRCIA readiness | AI Use Assessment | Evidence rung E3 incident notification; the anticipated-tier record |
| Safety systems | AI Risk Appetite | The consequence question at decision 2; the B3 candidate of AI changing setpoints without approval |
